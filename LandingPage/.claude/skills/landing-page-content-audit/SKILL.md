# Landing Page Content Audit — SaaS / Tech (CRO)

## When to apply

- **Automatically** after completing any SaaS landing page copy task.
- When user requests: "check content", "review copy", "audit landing page", "kiểm tra content".
- Before declaring any landing page content task "done".

---

## Audit process (complete ALL steps, skip none)

### Step 1: Identify scope

List all sections created or modified:
- [ ] Hero section (headline, subheadline, CTA)
- [ ] Problem / Pain section
- [ ] Solution / Feature section
- [ ] Social proof (testimonials, logos, numbers)
- [ ] Pricing section
- [ ] FAQ section
- [ ] Final CTA / Footer CTA
- [ ] Meta title & meta description

---

### Step 2: Messaging hierarchy — the 5-second test

**Hero section must answer 3 questions within 5 seconds of landing:**

| Question | Must be answered by |
|---|---|
| What does this product do? | Headline or subheadline |
| Who is it for? | Headline, subheadline, or hero body |
| What outcome do I get? | Headline — outcome-first, not feature-first |

**Headline checklist (SaaS-specific):**
- [ ] Leads with **outcome or job-to-be-done**, not with the product category.
  - ❌ "An advanced project management platform"
  - ✅ "Ship projects on time — without the status meeting chaos"
- [ ] Under 10 words preferred; 12 words absolute maximum.
- [ ] Zero buzzwords: "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "next-gen", "AI-powered" (unless AI is the core differentiator with proof).
- [ ] Passes the **"so what?" test** — a prospect cannot shrug it off.
- [ ] Passes the **"who cares?" test** — only your ICP nods, not everyone.

**Subheadline checklist:**
- [ ] Expands the headline — does NOT repeat it.
- [ ] Names the mechanism (how it works) OR names the ICP (who it's for) — whichever the headline omitted.
- [ ] 1–2 sentences maximum, no run-ons.

---

### Step 3: Copywriting / Messaging quality

**Clarity:**
- [ ] 8th-grade reading level or below.
- [ ] No passive voice in key selling statements.
- [ ] No filler: "In today's fast-paced world", "Leverage synergies", "End-to-end solution", "Seamlessly integrates".
- [ ] Every feature is paired with the outcome it produces for the user:
  - ❌ "Real-time analytics dashboard"
  - ✅ "See what's working the moment it happens — no waiting for weekly reports"

**Specificity (SaaS must be numbers-driven):**
- [ ] Quantified claims wherever possible:
  - ❌ "Saves you time"
  - ✅ "Saves ops teams 4 hours/week on manual reporting"
- [ ] Social proof numbers are specific, not rounded:
  - ❌ "2,000+ customers"
  - ✅ "2,347 teams across 40 countries"
- [ ] Testimonials include: full name + job title + company — no anonymous quotes.

**SaaS-specific messaging checks:**
- [ ] Onboarding friction addressed: "Set up in 10 minutes", "No dev work required", "Import from X in 1 click".
- [ ] Integration story present if integrations are a key value prop (show logos, not just "100+ integrations").
- [ ] Security / compliance mentioned if targeting enterprise or regulated industries (SOC 2, GDPR, etc.).
- [ ] Free trial / freemium boundary is crystal clear — zero ambiguity about what is free vs paid.

**Transformation arc:**
- [ ] Before state (pain) → After state (outcome) arc is present somewhere on the page.
- [ ] The reader feels "this is written for someone like me" within the first scroll.

---

### Step 4: CTA (Call-to-Action) effectiveness

**Primary CTA copy:**
- [ ] Action verb + value, never generic:
  - ❌ "Submit", "Get Started", "Learn More", "Click Here"
  - ✅ "Start My Free Trial", "See It In Action", "Book a 20-min Demo"
- [ ] CTA copy matches the offer exactly — if it's a demo, say demo; if it's a trial, say trial.
- [ ] Micro-copy directly below CTA kills the #1 objection at that stage:
  - Trial: "No credit card required. Cancel anytime."
  - Demo: "30-minute call. No sales pressure."
  - Freemium: "Free forever on the Starter plan."

**CTA placement (CRO standard):**
- [ ] CTA present **above the fold** in hero.
- [ ] CTA repeated after social proof section (trust established → strike while intent is high).
- [ ] CTA present in **final section** (last chance before exit).
- [ ] No section longer than 2 viewport-heights without a CTA or directional cue.
- [ ] Single dominant primary CTA per section — no 3 competing buttons.

**CTA friction reduction:**
- [ ] Form fields: minimum viable — ask only what is absolutely needed at this stage.
  - Trial signup: email only (or email + password max).
  - Demo booking: name + email + company (use Calendly over custom form).
- [ ] No CAPTCHA on primary CTA form if avoidable.
- [ ] SSO / OAuth option (Sign in with Google) if technically feasible — removes password friction.

---

### Step 5: Social proof audit

**Presence:**
- [ ] At least 2 forms of social proof on page (from: testimonials, logo wall, case study stats, G2/Capterra rating badge, customer count).

**Testimonial quality:**
- [ ] Each testimonial addresses a **specific outcome or objection**, not generic praise:
  - ❌ "Great product, highly recommend!"
  - ✅ "We cut monthly close from 5 days to 1 day."
- [ ] Testimonials from companies in the ICP's industry (peer validation — not random verticals).
- [ ] Photo of the person included — measurably lifts trust.
- [ ] Full name + job title + company always present.

**Logo wall:**
- [ ] Recognizable / aspirational logos listed first (authority by association).
- [ ] Grouped under a trust headline: "Trusted by teams at…" or "Used by 500+ companies including…"

**Ratings:**
- [ ] Star rating cites the source platform: G2, Capterra, or Google — never self-reported.
- [ ] Format: ⭐ 4.8/5 · 1,200+ reviews on G2 (source + volume = credibility).

---

### Step 6: SEO fundamentals

- [ ] **Meta title**: 50–60 characters. Primary keyword near the front. Brand name at end.
- [ ] **Meta description**: 150–160 characters. Primary keyword + core benefit + CTA verb.
- [ ] **H1**: Exactly one H1 per page. Matches or closely mirrors meta title intent.
- [ ] **H2s**: Cover major sections. Secondary keywords included naturally — not stuffed.
- [ ] **Primary keyword**: In H1, first paragraph, and at least 2 H2s.
- [ ] **Image alt text**: All images have descriptive alt text — not "image1.png", not empty.
- [ ] **Internal links**: At least 1 link to a deeper resource (blog post, docs, case study).
- [ ] **Canonical tag**: Set correctly if page exists at multiple URLs.
- [ ] **FAQ schema**: Add if FAQ section exists (expands SERP real estate with rich results).
- [ ] **Core Web Vitals**: LCP under 2.5s, CLS under 0.1, FID/INP under 200ms.

---

### Step 7: Brand voice & tone

**Consistency:**
- [ ] Tone is consistent top-to-bottom — no formal hero → casual features → aggressive close.
- [ ] Pronouns: "you/your" for the reader; "we/our" for the company. Never "they" for own company.
- [ ] One voice: confident but not arrogant, clear but not dumbed-down, friendly but not slangy.

**SaaS voice anti-patterns (flag and rewrite immediately):**
- [ ] ❌ "We are passionate about…" — shows nothing; replace with a proof point.
- [ ] ❌ "Our mission is to…" — belongs on the About page, not the landing page.
- [ ] ❌ More than 10% of sentences ending in exclamation marks — reads as desperate.
- [ ] ❌ "Easy", "simple", "effortless" without proof — say HOW it's easy instead.
- [ ] ❌ Emoji in body copy unless brand voice explicitly calls for it.
- [ ] ❌ Jargon the ICP does not use themselves in their own job titles / tools.

**Locale (if Vietnamese or bilingual):**
- [ ] Natural colloquial Vietnamese tone — not translated-from-English stiffness.
- [ ] No mid-sentence language switching.
- [ ] Currency / date / number formats match target locale (VND, dd/mm/yyyy, dấu chấm ngàn).

---

### Step 8: Conversion flow logic

- [ ] Page follows **AIDA** or **PAS** narrative structure end-to-end:
  - **AIDA**: Attention (hero) → Interest (problem/features) → Desire (social proof/pricing) → Action (CTA)
  - **PAS**: Problem → Agitate (make pain vivid) → Solution (your product)
- [ ] No section is a dead end — each flows logically into the next.
- [ ] FAQ placed **before** the final CTA (handles last objections right before the ask).
- [ ] Pricing (if present):
  - [ ] Most popular plan visually highlighted.
  - [ ] Pricing anchoring: show highest plan first to make mid-tier feel affordable.
  - [ ] Annual/monthly toggle if annual discount ≥ 15%.
  - [ ] Per-seat or usage pricing explained plainly — no calculator required to estimate cost.
- [ ] No content that creates new confusion without immediately resolving it.

---

### Step 9: i18n content audit

Run this step whenever the page supports more than 1 locale. Skip only if the product is confirmed single-language with no future i18n plans.

**String completeness:**
- [ ] Every user-facing string on the page is externalized — no hardcoded text in templates/components.
- [ ] Translation files exist for **all** supported locales — no locale is missing keys that others have.
- [ ] Missing keys fall back gracefully (default locale shown, not a raw key like `hero.headline`).
- [ ] Pluralization rules handled per locale — do not use `count > 1 ? "items" : "item"` logic in code; use i18n plural forms (e.g. `i18next` plurals, Laravel `trans_choice`).

**String quality per locale:**
- [ ] Strings are **adapted**, not literally translated:
  - ❌ Vietnamese: "Bắt đầu dùng thử miễn phí của tôi" (robot-stiff)
  - ✅ Vietnamese: "Dùng thử miễn phí ngay" (natural, colloquial)
- [ ] CTA copy re-adapted per locale — not word-for-word translation of the English CTA.
- [ ] Idioms and cultural references replaced, not translated (e.g. baseball metaphors → football/soccer for non-US markets).
- [ ] No placeholder text left in any locale: "Lorem ipsum", "TODO: translate", "[MISSING]".

**Dynamic content:**
- [ ] Numbers formatted per locale:
  - EN: 1,200.50 | VI: 1.200,50 | DE: 1.200,50
- [ ] Dates formatted per locale:
  - EN: March 27, 2026 | VI: 27 tháng 3, 2026
- [ ] Currency formatted with correct symbol and position:
  - USD: $99/mo | VND: 99.000 ₫/tháng (symbol after number in Vietnamese)
- [ ] Phone number format matches locale (Vietnam: +84 xx xxxx xxxx).

**String expansion / contraction (layout safety):**
- [ ] UI tested with the longest locale string — German and Vietnamese can run 30–40% longer than English.
- [ ] Button text never truncates or overflows at any supported locale.
- [ ] Headline does not break to 4+ lines in any locale at 375px mobile.
- [ ] Fixed-height containers do not clip text in verbose locales.

**SEO per locale:**
- [ ] Each locale has its own `<meta title>` and `<meta description>` — not the same English tags served to all.
- [ ] `<html lang="...">` matches the page locale exactly (`lang="vi"`, `lang="en"`, `lang="ja"`, etc.).
- [ ] `hreflang` tags present and correct on all locale variants:
  ```html
  <link rel="alternate" hreflang="en" href="https://example.com/en/" />
  <link rel="alternate" hreflang="vi" href="https://example.com/vi/" />
  <link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://example.com/" />
  ```
- [ ] `x-default` hreflang points to the correct fallback locale.
- [ ] Each locale served at its own URL — not via `?lang=vi` query param (bad for SEO indexing).
- [ ] Sitemap includes all locale URLs.

**RTL support (if Arabic, Hebrew, or Persian targeted):**
- [ ] `dir="rtl"` set on `<html>` or per-section element for RTL locales.
- [ ] CSS uses logical properties (`margin-inline-start` not `margin-left`) OR RTL stylesheet provided.
- [ ] Icons and directional elements (arrows, chevrons) are mirrored for RTL.
- [ ] Form input alignment correct in RTL.

---

### Step 10: Summary

- List all content violations found.
- Prioritize severity:
  - 🔴 **Critical** — kills conversion (missing CTA, vague headline, wrong offer framing)
  - 🟡 **Major** — hurts conversion (weak testimonials, no micro-copy, buzzword-heavy features)
  - 🟢 **Minor** — polish (tone inconsistency, SEO tag length off by 5 chars)
- Fix Critical and Major immediately.
- Report to user anything requiring a strategic decision (ICP repositioning, pricing display, offer change).
- Confirm: **messaging, copy, SEO, i18n, and CTA are aligned and conversion-ready**.
